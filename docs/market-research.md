# Peak: market research brief (October 2026)

Every figure below is also in `src/data/facts.js` with its source. Change it there, not in the slides.

## 1. The global figure (replaces the unsourced "$14B by 2027")

- Global e-commerce platform market: **$9.07 billion in 2025, $16.5 billion by 2030 (12.7% CAGR)**. MarketsandMarkets, via ResearchAndMarkets (Business Wire, Aug 2025).
- Caveat: analyst firms disagree because they define the market differently (one lists "e-commerce software" at $6.1B in 2023). Show it as one estimate, and never as the market Peak "will capture".
- Peak is broader than a store builder (storefront, bookings, customers, costing, email, tasks), so this is the closest published category, not a perfect match. The old $14B figure had no source and does not match any current estimate.
- Shopify's share of the platform market is quoted at about 26% in the same analyst material. Treat that as context for the competition slide, not a hard number, until checked against a primary source.

## 2. Canada (the sourced replacement for "3.8 million")

| Figure | Number | Source |

| Employer businesses | 1,099,521 | ISED, Key Small Business Statistics 2025 (Dec 2024) |
| Small employer businesses (1-99 employees) | **1,079,188 (98.2%)** | same |
| Businesses with no employees, revenue above $30K | **3.48 million** | Statistics Canada, Canadian business counts, Dec 2024 |
| Alberta small employer businesses | 137,182 | ISED |
| Small businesses with online sales | 32% (2021) | Statistics Canada, SDTIU 2021 |

The old "3.8 million" was most likely employer plus non-employer businesses. Today's equivalent is about 4.56 million (1.08M + 3.48M), but the two numbers come from different counting methods (enterprise level vs location level), so do not add them on a slide. Quote them separately.

## 3. Peak's segment (small employer businesses by industry, Dec 2024, ISED Table 3)

| Industry | Small employer businesses |
|---|---|
| Professional, scientific and technical services | 150,105 |
| Health care and social assistance | 119,783 |
| Other services (salons, repair, personal care) | 103,576 |
| Retail trade | 96,799 |
| Manufacturing (makers) | 44,440 |
| Arts, entertainment and recreation | 16,772 |
| **Total** | **531,475** |

This is an **upper bound by industry**. It includes businesses Peak does not fit (heavy manufacturing, care homes) and leaves out sole operators, who are probably a large share of Peak's real customers. A tighter count needs the non-employer split by industry (StatCan table 33-10-0765) and a decision on which sub-industries (NAICS 4-digit) count as "makers" and "service providers".

## 4. What it means for the stages

- Stage 3 (4,000 paying merchants) is **0.75%** of the 531,475 industry group, **0.37%** of all small employer businesses, and about **0.09%** if sole operators are included.
- Theoretical ceiling: if every business in the industry group paid, subscriptions would be about **$191M a year at $30/month** or **$274M at the $43 mix**. Present this only as a ceiling, never as a forecast.
- Online-selling adoption (32% of small businesses in 2021) supports the story that most small businesses are still not selling online, but the data is five years old. Statistics Canada has a 2024 table (33-10-0925) worth pulling before a final deck.

## 4b. Scale check: Shopify stores in Canada

- Third-party trackers count about **131,000 to 170,000 active Shopify stores in Canada** (StoreLeads; the higher figure is a 2026-06-01 pull reported by eightx.co). They differ because of date and definition. These are estimates, not Shopify's own numbers.
- Stage 3 (4,000 paying merchants) is **about 2.3% to 3.1%** of that range.
- Apparel is about 20% of Canadian Shopify stores; beauty and fitness is about 10% in the city-level reports.
- Do not use the global "5.5 million Shopify stores" figure from search summaries: Shopify does not publish a store count, and the figure comes from third-party blogs.
